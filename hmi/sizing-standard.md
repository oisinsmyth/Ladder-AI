# Physical sizing standard — millimetres first, pixels derived

**Added 2026-08-17** on the owner's instruction: *"calculate approx. what size in mm buttons and
interactable objects are on the real screen, and have some standards on sizing."*

Feeds **Lane A** (`docs/17-hmi-conventions.md`) as draft rules, and **Lane B** (T2's touch-target
check) as config rather than constants.

---

## The principle — and this repo already reached it independently

> **Express every physical threshold in millimetres, store panel px/mm as config, derive pixels.
> Never hard-code 48.**
> — `docs/notes/hmi-web-tooling-research.md`, from the Unified panel-geometry work

A pixel is not a unit of ergonomics. A finger is about 9 mm wide on every panel this project will
ever touch; **48 px is 9 mm on exactly one of them.**

## Why this bites us specifically, and it is not a rounding error

The anchor is a **KTP900 Basic**. The first application is a **Basic 7"**. Both are **800×480** — so
a layout transfers pixel-for-pixel between them, with no reflow, no scaling, and no visible warning
that anything has changed.

**But the same 48 px button is 9.1 mm on the 7" and 11.7 mm on the 9".** The pixels are identical
and the physical object is 29% larger.

🔴 **Which means the anchor is the forgiving one and the target is the strict one.** Design against
the 9" anchor and everything is undersized by a quarter when it reaches the 7" panel it was actually
for — with every mechanical check still green, because in pixels nothing changed at all. This is a
`MemoryLayout`-class trap: invisible to every gate that does not know about millimetres.

**Rule: design to the smallest physical panel in the family. Here that is the 7".**

### 🔴 CORRECTION — Basic panels do NOT have square pixels, and this document previously said they did

The first draft asserted *"square pixels on both, so one px/mm figure serves both axes"*, reasoning
from the **MTP700** (Unified), whose 152 × 91 mm really is 1.670 against 800/480 = 1.667.

**The datasheets say the Basic panels are different.** KTP700 Basic is 154.1 × 85.9 mm — an aspect
of **1.794**, i.e. 16:9 physical carrying a 5:3 pixel grid. KTP900 Basic is 1.773. So a pixel is
**wider than it is tall** on both, by about 7.7% on the 7" and 6.4% on the 9".

**Consequence: a square in pixels is not a square in millimetres.** A 100 × 100 px button on the
anchor is **24.8 mm wide and 23.3 mm tall**. Small, but it means a single px/mm constant is wrong in
one axis whichever value you pick, and the error runs the wrong way — the *vertical* axis is the
tighter one, and height is what a touch target usually has least of.

**So px/mm is a PAIR, not a scalar**, and the minor-axis check must use the vertical figure. This is
exactly the class of thing `target-differences.md` exists to catch: it was a plausible inference from
a verified neighbouring panel, and it was wrong.

## The bands

Thresholds in millimetres, pixels derived per panel. **The px columns are outputs, not inputs.**

Pixels given as **H × V**, because the pixels are not square (see the correction above).

🔴 **THIS TABLE IS GENERATED BY THE TOOL — `hmi-cli panels --bands`. Do not retype it.**

| band | mm | KTP700 (7", target) | KTP900 (9", anchor) | use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| absolute minimum interactive | **9** | **47 × 51 px** | 37 × 39 px | never smaller, anything touchable |
| standard button | **12** | **63 × 68 px** | 49 × 52 px | the default for a command |
| primary / frequent action | **15** | **78 × 84 px** | 61 × 65 px | start, stop, ack, mode |
| safety-critical | **20** | **104 × 112 px** | 81 × 86 px | STOP / E-STOP |
| minimum gap between interactives | **3** | 16 × 17 px | 13 × 13 px | hard floor |
| recommended gap | **5** | 26 × 28 px | 21 × 22 px | the default |

### 🔴 Correction — the first version of this table was wrong in six cells

It was computed by hand with **rounding**; the checker uses **`Ceiling`**. For a *minimum*
threshold, ceiling is the only correct choice: 50 px at 5.59 px/mm is **8.95 mm**, which is below a
9 mm floor. Rounding down produces a threshold that does not enforce the rule it names.

Found by a unit test asserting the tool's arithmetic, not by re-reading the table — a table nobody
can check is a table that drifts from the checker enforcing it. **Hence the generation rule above:
`hmi-cli panels --bands` is the source, and this section is a transcript of it.**

### ✅ Corroborated against real engineering practice on the anchor

Three classes of interactive object were measured on the anchor's main screen (structural geometry
only — no restricted content reproduced, per `docs/13-data-boundary.md`):

| class | pixels | on the 9" anchor | verdict against the bands |
|---|---|---|---|
| main action buttons | 100 × 100 | 24.8 × 23.3 mm | **well clear** — above even the 20 mm safety band |
| navigation buttons | 117 × 40 | 29.0 × 9.3 mm | **9.3 mm minor axis — just clears the 9 mm floor** |
| switches | 64 × 22 | 15.8 × **5.1 mm** | 🔴 **below the floor, by a factor of 1.8** |

**Two of three classes independently corroborate the 9 mm floor** — one comfortably, one landing
within 0.3 mm of it, which is the kind of agreement that suggests a real constraint rather than a
borrowed number. The third **violates it**, and it is the fiddliest control on the screen.

That is the standard earning its keep on first contact: it agrees with the experienced engineer
where they were deliberate, and disagrees where the default control size was simply accepted.
*(A caveat worth keeping: this is the 9" panel. The same switch on the 7" target would be 4.2 mm.)*

**Gloved operation moves everything up one band.** Worth settling with the engineer early — it is a
site fact, not a design choice, and it changes the whole layout budget.

### The 9 mm floor, and an honest note on its provenance

9 mm square (11 mm circular) for error rates under 4%, with 2–3 mm minimum spacing, is attributed to
**ISO 9241-411** — but this repo tried to verify that against the standard itself and **could not**
(`docs/notes/hmi-web-tooling-research.md`, could-not-establish #24). It is reported by secondary
sources and it agrees with Apple's 44 pt, Material's 48 dp and Lighthouse's 48 px, which is
reassuring convergence rather than proof.

Treat 9 mm as **a well-corroborated floor of uncertain pedigree**, not as a cited standard, and do
not write "per ISO 9241-411" into the conventions document.

## What this does to the layout budget

At 800×480 with a 40 px title bar and a 60 px alarm banner, the usable field is **800 × 380**.

With 12 mm standard buttons on the 7" target — 64 px plus a 27 px recommended gap, so a 91 px pitch —
that field holds **8 columns × 4 rows**. Comfortably fewer once labels, values and grouping are real.

That is the honest information budget for the first application, and it is the number Lane A should
design the hierarchy against. It is emphatically not "experiment 2's screen, smaller".

## Draft rules for Lane A

Numbering is Lane A's to assign; the content is what matters.

- **H-S1** Every interactive object is **≥ 9 mm** on its minor axis, measured on the *smallest*
  panel in the target family. `[MECHANIZED]`
- **H-S2** Commands default to **12 mm**; primary/frequent actions to **15 mm**. `[MECHANIZED]`
- **H-S3** Safety-critical controls (STOP / E-STOP) are **≥ 20 mm**. Pairs with the red-STOP
  carve-out — the two together are the physical-safety rule. `[MECHANIZED]`
- **H-S4** Interactive objects are separated by **≥ 3 mm**, **5 mm** by default. `[MECHANIZED]`
- **H-S5** Thresholds are **declared in millimetres and converted using the target panel's px/mm**.
  A hard-coded pixel threshold is a defect regardless of its value. `[MECHANIZED]` — this is the one
  that keeps the others honest across panels.
- **H-S6** The panel's px/mm **travels with the artifact** (in `screen-ir.json`, alongside the target
  family). A screen IR that does not state the panel it was sized for cannot be checked. `[MECHANIZED]`

## For Lane B (T2)

Touch-target checking takes **px/mm as config, keyed on panel model**, and reports findings **in mm
with the px in parentheses** — `7.4 mm (39 px) — below the 9 mm floor` — because "39 px" alone is
unactionable when the reader does not know the panel.

**A screen IR with no panel declared is a refusal, not a default to 5.26.** Silently assuming a panel
is how the 29% error above arrives with every check green.
