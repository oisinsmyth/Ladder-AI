An accent is a marker, not a fill

Two colour systems share this screen and must not be confused. The site's blue themes the chrome. Green, red and light blue mark which command is which. Neither ever says what the plant is doing — that channel stays free for alarms.

Authored HTML vs the panel's own data

The right-hand image is not a second render of the source. It is redrawn from the SimaticML TIA returned on re-export — every rectangle, colour, position and label read out of the project.

Distinct from both, and from every alarm hue.

Each command button is a grey body lifted from the field with a 12 px bar down its leading edge — 8% of the control. H-109 caps that at 15%: above it, the colour stops reading as a label and becomes a region competing with the alarm palette.

The accents are static. They are identical whether the plant is running, stopped or faulted, which is exactly why they are safe: they carry no state, so the eye stops reporting them and the alarm channel stays loud.

The first draft of this screen failed its own checker, and it was right to. The STOP button was 96 px — 17.2 mm on the minor axis, under the 20 mm floor for a safety-critical control. It is now 112 px. Three further findings were the checker applying rules written before the branding ones existed, and one was a bug in the rule itself: red sits on the 0° seam of the hue circle and the band check did not wrap, so it rejected a textbook red as "hue 5°, expects 340–375°".
